# CHEMBL141190 Binding to c-ABL Kinase Domain

## 1. Binding Mode

This compound likely occupies the **ATP-binding pocket** of the c-ABL kinase domain, competing with natural substrate ATP. The thiophene ring system provides the core scaffold for positioning within the hydrophobic kinase pocket, while the chloroacetyl substituents extend into neighboring binding subsites.

## 2. Key Functional Groups & Roles

| Group | Role |
|-------|------|
| **Thiophene ring** | Core scaffold; π-π stacking with conserved aromatic residues (e.g., phenylalanines in DFG motif) |
| **Chloroacetyl groups (×2)** | Electrophilic warheads; potential Michael acceptors for nucleophilic cysteine residues; enhance lipophilicity |
| **Carbonyl oxygens** | H-bond acceptors; coordinate with backbone NH groups in kinase hinge region |
| **Chlorine atoms** | Increase polarity; facilitate halogen bonding with polar residues |

## 3. Mechanism of Action

**Type I/II reversible kinase inhibitor** (non-covalent predominantly, though chloroacetyl groups suggest potential for covalent modification with reactive cysteines)

- Blocks ATP binding → prevents phosphorylation → inhibits downstream BCR-ABL signaling
- Relevant context: STI-571 (imatinib) in the same PDB structure is a prototypical BCR-ABL inhibitor

## 4. Strengths & Weaknesses

### Strengths:
- Moderate lipophilicity (LogP 2.59) → reasonable membrane permeability
- Compact molecular weight (237 Da) → favorable for bioavailability
- Dual electrophilic sites → potential for enhanced potency via covalent engagement

### Weaknesses:
- **Very weak inhibition (10%)** → Poor binding affinity relative to imatinib
- Missing key pharmacophore elements (likely lacks the critical hinge-binding NH or polar groups for high-affinity binding)
- Symmetric bis-chloroacetyl design lacks asymmetric specificity
- No obvious H-bond donors → reduced polar interactions with kinase hinge
- High electrophilicity may cause off-target reactivity and metabolic instability

## 5. Optimization Strategies

1. **Add hinge-binder moieties**: Incorporate NH-containing groups (amides, imidazoles) to H-bond with backbone carbonyls at the kinase hinge—standard for potent kinase inhibitors

2. **Asymmetrize the scaffold**: Replace one chloroacetyl with a polar group (NH-pyridine, hydroxyl) to exploit specific pocket contacts

3. **Enhance selectivity**: Introduce substituents exploiting ABL-specific residues (e.g., hydrophobic groups in the selectivity pocket)

4. **Reduce electrophilicity**: Replace reactive chloroacetyl with stable carbonyl derivatives to minimize off-target covalent binding and improve selectivity/safety

5. **Conformational constraint**: Fuse the thiophene core into a polycyclic scaffold to pre-organize for kinase binding

**Conclusion**: This compound shows the basic structural scaffold but lacks potency-critical interactions. Systematic medicinal chemistry improvements toward established ABL kinase inhibitor pharmacophores are needed.
